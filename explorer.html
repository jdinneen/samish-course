Samish Bay shellfish closures, 2008–2026

Samish Bay (Skagit County, Washington) is an oyster-growing area. When rain washes animal and human waste down the Samish River, harvest is shut until river water tests clean. These charts combine the three spreadsheets you downloaded with the Lab's copy of the river-flow gauge record.

How the closure rule works

1 · The river jumpsIn the 2020–26 closure notices, if the Samish River's flow rises fast in 24 hours (measured at the U.S. Geological Survey, or "USGS", gauge 12201500 near Burlington), the county closes the "Conditionally Approved" part of the bay right away. Flow is in cfs (cubic feet of water per second).
2 · Closed while testingStaff sample the river for fecal coliform, a bacteria group that signals animal or human waste. Results come back as a count per 100 mL of water.
3 · Reopen on a clean "load"Load = flow × bacteria count, i.e. the total bacteria the river carries into the bay per day. At or below 4.74 trillion the sample passes. A pass usually lets the bay reopen, but not automatically: after a failed sample the bay stays closed at least 48 hours, and a pass doesn't count if the river starts rising again. A flood closes the whole bay.

What decides pass or fail?

Each dot is one river sample: river flow across, bacteria count up (both on multiplying scales, so each gridline is ×10). The dashed line is the 4.74-trillion load limit. Above it fails, below it passes. At high flow, even a modest bacteria count fails, because the river is carrying so much water. That's built into the formula (flow is one of the numbers multiplied), so "high flow fails more" is arithmetic, not a discovery.
